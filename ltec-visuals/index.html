Lunar Thermal Energy Conversion

A heat engine across a crater

A working fluid is pumped between a permanently shadowed crater floor and a sunlit rim. The temperature gap drives a turbine — and the generator powers the base.

The cycle

1
Pump drives cold liquid out of the shadowed crater toward the light.
2
Evaporator on the sunlit rim boils the fluid into high-pressure gas.
3
Turbine & generator — the gas expands, spins the turbine, makes electricity.
4
Condenser back in the PSR cools the gas to liquid, and the loop repeats.

Scales to ~2575 kW over 2500 m² of pipe — about 29× the ISS solar arrays. Geometry is illustrative; temperatures & efficiency follow the TexFlow LTEC paper (points A & C, Shackleton Crater rim).
