Lunar Thermal Energy Conversion

One engine per crater

Instead of ringing a single crater, each LTEC unit claims its own small permanently shadowed crater. Five nearby units are daisy-chained into one grid, and a single cord carries the combined output to a lunar base.

How the field works

1
Each unit sits on one crater's rim, condenser dipping into its own shadow.
2
Fluid boils on the sunlit rim, spins a turbine & generator, condenses in the cold.
3
A daisy-chain cable ties the five units into one shared micro-grid.
4
One cord carries the combined output to the lunar base.

Power is practical output: Carnot caps a 10 m² unit at 10.3 kW, and a real organic Rankine engine reaches about 44% of that — 4.6 kW. Five of them give ~23 kW.
