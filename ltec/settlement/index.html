Lunar Thermal Energy Conversion

Powering a lunar settlement

Several permanently shadowed craters each carry their own swarm of LTEC units. Every swarm feeds a shared grid that converges on a central power-storage hub at the heart of a lunar settlement built for roughly a thousand residents.

How the grid converges

1
Each crater rim carries a swarm of LTEC units dipping condensers into its shadow.
2
Sunlit rims boil the fluid, spin the turbines, and the cold floors condense it back.
3
Units in a crater share a local power ring; one feeder cord runs inward.
4
Feeders converge on the central storage hub buffering the settlement grid.

Power is practical output: Carnot caps a 10 m² unit at 10.3 kW, and a real organic Rankine engine reaches about 44% of that — 4.6 kW. Nineteen of them give ~87 kW.
